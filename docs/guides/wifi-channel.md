# Wi-Fi networks share your Frame's channel

Your Frame and its wireless adapter talk over one Wi-Fi channel. Other Wi-Fi networks nearby are using the same channel, or one that overlaps it. Only one device can send at a time on a channel, so these networks take turns with your Frame. That can cause brief hiccups in your stream.

## What to do

1. **Check whether your Frame can use 6 GHz.** If DongleDoctor also says "Your Frame isn't using 6 GHz", fix that first ([see how](wifi-band.md)). 6 GHz is usually far quieter, and this warning often goes away on its own.
2. **If one of the networks listed is yours, move your router to another channel.** Open your router's settings (usually through its app, or by typing its address into a browser) and change the Wi-Fi channel for that band. Choose a channel outside the range DongleDoctor shows for your Frame.
3. **Run DongleDoctor again** to check the result.

## Good to know

- **Neighbours' networks are out of your control.** You can't move them, but switching your Frame to 6 GHz usually avoids them.
- **Weaker networks matter less.** DongleDoctor only warns about networks strong enough to compete for airtime.
- **Not everything shows up.** DongleDoctor sees Wi-Fi networks only. Other things that use the same radio waves, like Bluetooth or microwave ovens, don't appear in the list.
